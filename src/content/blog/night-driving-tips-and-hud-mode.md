Visibility drops at night, fatigue builds and glare adds stress. A few habits make a big difference.

## Before you go

- **Clean your windscreen, mirrors and headlights.** Dirty glass scatters light.
- **Check your lights.** Replace dim or failed bulbs, and make sure beams are aligned.
- **Rest first.** Do not start a long night drive when tired.
- **Plan breaks.** Stop every two hours or when you feel drowsy.

## On the road

1. **Slow down.** You can only stop within the distance your headlights show.
2. **Increase your gap.** Reaction time matters more when you see less.
3. **Dip your mirror** to reduce glare from cars behind.
4. **Look to the edge of the road** when facing oncoming headlights, rather than into them.
5. **Dim your dashboard** to keep your eyes adjusted.

## Can heads-up displays work well during nighttime driving?

Yes, night is where a heads-up display (HUD) helps most. In a dark car, a large reflected speed reading is quicker to glance at than a small dial, and your eyes stay closer to the road. Two things matter:

- **Brightness.** A HUD that is too bright causes glare and ruins your night vision. Keep it dim.
- **Accuracy.** A phone HUD uses GPS speed, which is close on open roads but can lag in tunnels and dense cities. Your car's speedometer is still the reference.

## Where a speed HUD helps

Road Rank Pro includes a HUD mode: a big speed display, with a mirror option so it reflects correctly on a windscreen. In a dark car it gives you a quick glance at your speed without hunting for a small dial.

Use it only when your phone is mounted safely and the display is set up before you move off. Never handle your phone while driving.

## Safety first

HUD mode is a convenience, not a substitute for your vehicle's instruments, and speed shown is an estimate from GPS. Set it up at a standstill, or ask a passenger.

## What to expect in your score

Night drives are good for smoothness practice. With less traffic you can build the habit of lifting off early and braking gently.
