Driving apps rely on your phone's GPS and motion sensors. They are good, but not perfect.

## What can affect accuracy

- **Tall buildings and tunnels.** Signal reflections or loss cause jumps and gaps.
- **Weather and tree cover.** Heavy cloud or dense trees weaken the signal.
- **Phone placement.** A loose phone sliding around registers movement that is not the car's.
- **Cold starts.** The first fix can take a moment, especially after a long time off.
- **Battery saver modes.** Aggressive power saving can reduce location updates.

## Set up for best results

1. **Mount the phone securely** in a holder, not in a cup or on a seat.
2. **Start the drive before you move.** Wait for a GPS signal if the app shows it is searching.
3. **Allow precise location** for the app.
4. **Turn off battery restrictions** for the app if recording stops.
5. **Keep the screen awake if needed.** Road Rank keeps it on while a drive records.

## Why scores differ between similar drives

Real driving varies: traffic, lights and other drivers change from day to day. Sensor noise adds a small amount of randomness. That is why averages over several drives are more meaningful than any one result.

## Treat numbers as estimates

Speed is calculated from GPS and may differ slightly from your vehicle's speedometer. Use it for feedback, not as a replacement for your car's instruments.

## Do not interact while driving

Start, pause and review the app when stopped, or let a passenger do it.
