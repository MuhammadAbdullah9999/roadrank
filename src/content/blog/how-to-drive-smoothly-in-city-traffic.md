City driving is full of lights, junctions and sudden stops, so it is where smoothness matters most.

## 1. Read the light sequence

Watch how long a green has been green and whether pedestrians are waiting. A "stale" green is about to change, so ease off early.

## 2. Coast to red lights

If the light ahead is red, lift off and roll. By the time you arrive, it may have turned green and you do not need to stop at all.

## 3. Keep a bigger gap

A bigger gap absorbs the stop-start wave. You will brake less and more gently.

## 4. Accelerate progressively

Do not race to the next red. Gentle acceleration costs little time in the city, because the next stop is never far.

## 5. Look two or three cars ahead

See brake lights early and react to them, rather than to the car right in front.

## 6. Anticipate bus stops and parked cars

Expect pull-outs and opening doors. Move over early and slow down in advance.

## 7. Slow before turns and roundabouts

Brake in a straight line before the corner. Hold a steady speed through the turn.

## 8. Plan your route

Set your route before you leave, or ask a passenger. Never fiddle with your phone while driving.

## What to expect in your score

Smooth city driving usually removes most hard-braking and quick-acceleration events, which are the two things that cost points in Road Rank. Compare your commute across a week and see how it changes.

## Stay safe

These tips are about smoothness. Always follow local laws and drive to the conditions.
