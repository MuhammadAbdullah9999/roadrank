Seeing a number is useful. Knowing what it means is better.

**Short answer:** on a 0 to 100 scale, **85 or higher is a good driving score** and 95 or higher is excellent. Below 70 means several harsh moments worth fixing.

## The driving score range (0 to 100)

In Road Rank, every drive starts at 100 and loses points for hard-braking and quick-acceleration events. So the number tells you roughly how many of those events happened, relative to the drive.

| Score | Roughly means |
|---|---|
| 95 to 100 | Very smooth, almost no hard events |
| 85 to 94 | Smooth, a couple of events |
| 70 to 84 | Some harsh moments worth reviewing |
| Below 70 | Several hard events. Look at what caused them |

These bands are a guide, not an official rating.

## Is 80 a good driving score?

An 80 is fair, not bad. In Road Rank it usually means four to six harsh moments on that drive, for example three hard brakes and three fast take-offs. Two habits move most people from 80 into the 90s:

1. **Leave a three-second gap** to the car ahead, so you can slow down gently instead of braking hard.
2. **Roll on the accelerator** when pulling away from lights rather than pressing it quickly.

See [how to reduce quick acceleration](/blog/how-to-reduce-quick-acceleration) and [hard braking explained](/blog/hard-braking-explained) for more detail.

## What about 90 and 100?

A 90 means one or two events: a very smooth drive. A 100 means no hard braking or quick acceleration at all. Scoring 100 on a short, quiet drive is common. Doing it on a long commute in traffic is the real achievement.

## Why scores vary

- **Traffic.** Stop-start traffic produces more braking than an open road.
- **The route.** Junction-heavy streets are harder than a straight highway.
- **Other drivers.** A cut-in can force a hard stop.
- **Phone placement.** A loose phone can register false movement. Mount it securely.
- **Short drives.** A short trip can swing your score a lot, which is why leaderboards weight by distance.

## A realistic target

Look at your average over a week or a month rather than a single drive. Aim to raise that average by a few points, not to get 100 every time.

## Use the breakdown

The score is only half the story. The breakdown shows how many hard-braking and quick-acceleration events you had. Pick the one with the biggest count and work on it.

## What can you achieve when you drive smoothly?

Raising your score is not just about a number. Smooth driving usually brings:

- **Lower fuel use.** Hard acceleration burns fuel you then waste by braking. See [eco-driving tips to save fuel](/blog/eco-driving-tips-to-save-fuel).
- **Less wear.** Gentle braking makes brake pads and tyres last longer.
- **More margin for mistakes.** A bigger gap gives you time to react to other drivers.
- **Calmer passengers.** Nobody enjoys being thrown forward at every junction.

## Remember what it is

A phone-based score is an estimate for self-improvement. It is not a safety rating or an insurance score.
