Seeing a number is useful. Knowing what it means is better.

## The scale

In Road Rank, every drive starts at 100 and loses points for hard-braking and quick-acceleration events. So the number tells you roughly how many of those events happened, relative to the drive.

| Score | Roughly means |
|---|---|
| 95 to 100 | Very smooth, almost no hard events |
| 85 to 94 | Smooth, a couple of events |
| 70 to 84 | Some harsh moments worth reviewing |
| Below 70 | Several hard events. Look at what caused them |

These bands are a guide, not an official rating.

## Why scores vary

- **Traffic.** Stop-start traffic produces more braking than an open road.
- **The route.** Junction-heavy streets are harder than a straight highway.
- **Other drivers.** A cut-in can force a hard stop.
- **Phone placement.** A loose phone can register false movement. Mount it securely.
- **Short drives.** A short trip can swing your score a lot, which is why leaderboards weight by distance.

## A realistic target

Look at your average over a week or a month rather than a single drive. Aim to raise that average by a few points, not to get 100 every time.

## Use the breakdown

The score is only half the story. The breakdown shows how many hard-braking and quick-acceleration events you had. Pick the one with the biggest count and work on it.

## Remember what it is

A phone-based score is an estimate for self-improvement. It is not a safety rating or an insurance score.
