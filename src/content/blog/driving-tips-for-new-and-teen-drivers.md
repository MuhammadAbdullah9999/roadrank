The first years of driving are when habits form. Good ones last.

## Habits worth building from day one

- **Keep a big gap.** Three seconds is a good minimum, more in the wet.
- **Scan ahead.** Look well beyond the car in front, and check mirrors regularly.
- **Eliminate distractions.** Phone away, music set before you move off.
- **Brake early and gently.** Smooth stops are safer and calmer.
- **Obey speed limits.** Speeding reduces reaction time and increases stopping distance.
- **Never drive tired, or after drinking.**

## Using a score as a coach

A score gives feedback you can both talk about without it turning into a lecture. Look at the breakdown together. Which events happened? Where? What could be done differently?

## Tips for parents

1. **Agree on goals together.** Choose a target average rather than demanding perfection.
2. **Praise improvement.** Notice when the average rises.
3. **Avoid shaming.** A bad drive is a chance to learn.
4. **Share a private club.** Compare scores as a family challenge.
5. **Stay realistic.** Scores are estimates, not a measure of safety or a replacement for experience.

## Set the rules for phone use

The app should be started before the car moves, or by a passenger. Make it clear that the phone is never touched while driving.

## Experience matters most

Nothing replaces supervised practice in varied conditions: night, rain, motorways and busy streets. A score is a helpful extra, not the main teacher.
